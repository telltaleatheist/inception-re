# The Commodore 64 version's files

What the C64 files hold, from reading them beside DOS 1.03's. The disk images are
[.d64](d64.md). The Apple II holds the same maps (docs/versions.md, "Apple II"). Nothing here is imported yet, and the program (the 6502 code) is not read yet.

## The two disks

Disk 1 has 56 files, disk 2 has 97. Both are needed: the pictures, tiles and start-up files are
on disk 1 only; maps E–Z, all but one of the `T` files, most place scripts and place pictures
are on disk 2 only. Twelve names are on both. Ten are the same file (`BA`, `CBT`, `INT`,
`MA`–`MD`, `OR.ANM`, `SPEC`, `TB`); `WESTWOOD` and `BB` differ. Every file starts with its load
address (2 bytes); the sizes below leave it out.

## Pictures

| Files | What |
|---|---|
| `TPAG`, `TCHR`, `TCOL` | the title: a 320x200 multicolour bitmap (8,000 bytes), the screen colours (1,000) and the colour memory (1,000) |
| `EMBM`, `EMSM`, `EMCM` | the ending, the same form (not compared with DOS's yet) |
| `BSET`, `BCHR`, `BCOL` | the publisher's screen at start-up: a character set (2,048), a 40x25 screen (1,000) and its colours (1,000) |
| `DET.SCRN` | the 'Mech status screen: a 40x25 screen in the characters of `S0`'s first 2,048 bytes (1,001 bytes: 1,000 and one more) |

Checked by drawing each. In the bitmaps, the bit pairs 1 and 2 are the screen byte's high and low
nibbles and 3 is the colour memory. The background colour is a guess (black). It, and the
logo's two shared colours, are set by the program.

## Tiles

Each tile sheet is 256 tiles of 2x2 characters (16x16 pixels), in two files:

- `SETA`–`SETE`: a character set (256 characters of 8 bytes).
- `ICNA`–`ICND` (2,048 bytes): eight planes of 256 bytes. Planes 0–3 are the tile's four
  characters (top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right); planes 4–7 are their colour
  memory bytes. Colour bit 3 makes the character multicolour. The three shared colours
  are guesses in the drawings (green, brown, black).

| Sheet | Used for |
|---|---|
| `SETA` + `ICNA` | the towns (maps 1–10 and 12) |
| `SETB` + `ICNB` | map 11 (DOS: `DESTRUCT.ICN`) |
| `SETC` + `ICNC` | map 14, the cache (DOS: `STARLEAG.ICN`) |
| `SETD` + `ICND` | `MZ`, the star map |

Checked by drawing: each map is a clear picture with its own sheet and scrambled with the others.
`SETE` and the `COL` files (126 to 128 bytes) are not read.

## Maps

26 files `MA`–`MZ`, each 1,024 bytes: one 32x32 page, row by row, one tile number a cell.

| Pages | Map | How that is known |
|---|---|---|
| `MA`–`MD` | 1 (four quarters: top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right) | drawn; `TB`'s entrances are on door tiles |
| `ME`–`MH` | 11 | drawn: map 1 wrecked, in the other sheet |
| `MI`–`ML` | 2 | drawn; `TC`'s entrances are on door tiles |
| `MM`–`MT` | 3–10 in order | the entrances fit in that pairing (below) |
| `MU` | 12 | `TM`'s one entrance is at a building, and its script is DOS map 12's |
| `MV`–`MY` | 14 | 4,045 of 4,096 cells are DOS's |
| `MZ` | 15, the star map | drawn; not compared with DOS's `MAP15.MTP`, which has another form |

Pairing `MM`–`MT` with `TE`–`TL`: with the door tiles taken from maps 1 and 2 only, the
straight order puts 28 of 56 entrances on door tiles. Over all the orders the mean is 6
and 28 is the most, reached only by it and by changing `MO`–`MQ` among themselves. `MO`, `MP` and `MQ` have no such tiles, so the test says nothing about their
order. Drawn in the straight order, every entrance of `TG`, `TH` and `TI` is on plain ground beside a
building, 19 of 19; in any other pairing of the three, some entrances land on other tiles.
DOS map 13 (8x8, script 20) has no `T` file or page of its own.

### Compared with DOS

- **Map 14: 4,045 of 4,096 cells the same** (22, 14, 8 and 7 cells differ on `MV`, `MW`, `MX`
  and `MY`). 18 of the 51 are tile 0x1c for 0x35. Why they differ is not known.
- **Maps 1–11: different maps.** They are the same places with the same ingredients (map 1 has
  the same walled park with a lake, map 3 the same walled yard) but laid out anew: other
  buildings, at other cells, with other roads, and their own tile numbering. No page of them
  matches a DOS page at shifts of up to 3 cells (or, unshifted, turned or mirrored), allowing any
  renumbering of the tiles: the likeness to the best DOS page is hardly above that to the next
  best. Only `MK` (map 2's bottom left) scores clearly above the next best page, against DOS map
  2's bottom left shifted by 2; why (a walled yard is in both) is not looked into.
- **No entrance of any map is at DOS's position**, in place; six of 78 are within one cell of it.
- Map 12 is 32x32 cells where DOS's is 8x8, and is not compared.
- Map 15 is not read: the C64's star map is made of 32x32 tiles, DOS's of another form.

So a C64 import can't give DOS's maps 1–11 cell for cell.

## The `T` files: a map's people and entrances

`TB` is map 1, `TC` 2, `TD` 11, `TE`–`TL` 3–10, `TM` 12. Each is (bytes, from the load address on):

| Bytes | What |
|---|---|
| 0–7 | 0x80 and up in every file. Not read |
| 8–15 | not read |
| 16–23, 32–39 | shuffles of 0–8. In `TB`, `TD` and `TM` both are DOS's map 1 shuffle (+0x215) |
| 24–31 | the same 8 bytes in every file |
| 48–63 | 16 entrance x positions, in half-cells, as DOS's |
| 64–79 | 16 entrance y positions |
| 80–95 | 16 place script numbers, in DOS's numbering |
| 96– | the 8 townspeople's names, NUL-ended, upper case (none in `TD` and `TM`) |

There are no DOS second positions (+0x1c5, +0x1e5) and no place names here; where the C64 keeps
them is not known.

Compared with DOS: the scripts are the same list for maps 1, 2, 7, 9 and 12; for 3, 4, 5, 6 and 10 the
C64 has the same set of scripts with fewer repeated doors; map 11 has three entrances to DOS's one
(scripts 19, 19 and a third, 0, that may be a leftover); map 8 has script 15 (twice) where DOS has 3 and 4.
The names in place: 8 of 8 on maps 3 and 9; 7 on 1, 4 and 8; 6 on 6; 5 on 10; 2 on 7; none on 2 and 5
(there the people are others). Of the single differing names most are a spelling, the others another person.
